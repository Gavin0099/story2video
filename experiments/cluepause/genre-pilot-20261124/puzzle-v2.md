**Premise:** Retained: a buyer cuts a thread beneath a jeweler’s mechanical scale; the gold stays, but the reading drops. The seller is cheating. This is not a benevolent_misunderstanding twist.

**Reveal / author-only causal summary:** The thread is tied to the underside of the load-bearing pan, passes through the counter, and is pulled downward by the seller. The spring scale counts that extra force. Cutting the thread removes the force, not any gold.

## Author / host notes — never distribute to blind readers

The opening is the result; the spoken “Before the cut” at 7 s explicitly starts the earlier action. At 23 s the edit returns to the result, and the reveal reconstructs the earlier mechanism before replaying the cut. No EARLIER card is needed. Keep the same three gold pieces, rim dent, dial and counter hole in every view. Use a tabletop mechanical spring scale with a raised pan and visible clearance beneath it; the tether must attach to the moving pan, never the housing. The reference force is downward, not the mass of the thread.

Fair evidence before 25 s: real cut and lower reading by 0.9 s; unchanged gold at 3.5 s; taut thread through the counter at 11 s; a concealed, stationary fist at 15 s; seller’s interruption at 19 s. These support tampering without displaying a pull correlated with a rising needle. Neither the pan knot nor an active pull appears until 27 s. Do not add a hand-pull replay, arrows, a cutaway diagram or a narrator explanation beforehand.

Reasonable alternatives include a calibration linkage or another form of scale tampering. The setup need not make the exact attachment uniquely deducible. The reveal must physically show the tether bypassing the housing and loading the pan, so it resolves that ambiguity rather than introducing a different trick. Host core: external downward pull raises the reading; the cut removes that force while the gold remains. “The thread was heavy” is not sufficient.
