## 1. What is the video asking?
Why do shoes put away dry become wet only at the soles inside a locked cabinet in the morning?

## 2. Most likely answer
**90% confidence:** The automatic plant-watering system leaks or releases water from the hose loop inside the cabinet when its morning timer runs, filling the shoe tray from below.

Specific evidence: the timer clicks at six; its hose passes through the cabinet’s rear vent; water collects in the tray, wetting the soles while leaving the fabric and laces dry. The dry hallway makes water coming across the floor unlikely. The hose provides a route into the cabinet that does not require the key. I cannot tell whether the hose has a hole, a loose connection, or another fault.

## 3. Reasonable alternative explanations
- **7%:** Water overflows or leaks from the plant or reservoir above and drains into the cabinet through a gap. The watering schedule explains the morning timing, though this makes the hose loop less directly relevant.
- **3%:** Condensation accumulates overnight and pools beneath the shoes. This could explain the morning discovery and wet soles, but the timer and hose are much stronger evidence for a watering-system leak.

At 7–11 seconds, the dry laces and wet soles shifted my thinking from someone putting wet shoes back to water collecting underneath them. The watering timer at 15–19 seconds suggested a scheduled source; the hose entering the vent at 19–23 seconds made that explanation strongly likely.
