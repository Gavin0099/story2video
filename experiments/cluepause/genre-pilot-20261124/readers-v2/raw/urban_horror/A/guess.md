## 1. What is the main conflict or threat?

Something outside Nia’s locked door can imitate her sleeping mother, and Nia must avoid giving the listening intercom a reply whose final two words invite it inside.

## 2. The rules of this world as I understand them, and anything that seems inconsistent

- After midnight, the intercom records your first reply and keeps only its final two words.
- If those saved words constitute an invitation, the door unlocks.
- You cannot replace that recording until dawn, so a mistake seems irreversible for the night.
- The thing can copy Mom’s voice and even a cough she has just made inside the apartment. It seems able to hear the room without anyone pressing a button.
- The lock currently holds despite the handle moving, so the thing apparently still needs an invitation to enter.

I’m unsure what counts as a “reply”: any speech near the intercom, speech directed at it, or only Nia’s speech? Mom’s cough apparently does not count, but could she accidentally say something in her sleep? I also don’t know whether the final two words are interpreted without the rest of the sentence. That sounds like the trap, but it has not been demonstrated. Nothing feels like a definite contradiction yet; the boundaries are uncertain.

## 3. What I expect or fear will happen next, with reasoning

I expect Nia to blurt out something under pressure. The repeated attention to her mouth, her slipping hand, and the waiting light makes her next words feel dangerous.

My main fear is that she will say something intended to refuse entry, such as “Don’t come in,” and the intercom will save only “come in.” The two-word rule seems specifically designed to make an otherwise sensible response dangerous.

Alternatively, the copied voice might provoke her into answering her mother, or Mom might wake and speak before Nia can stop her. The cough makes me worry that the threat can monitor everything inside and adapt its imitation. I also wonder whether Nia could deliberately use an unmistakably safe first reply to use up the recording, although I don’t yet know whether that would protect her.

## 4. When did unease start, and what caused it?

Immediately: the mother’s voice comes through the intercom while the handle moves without Nia touching it. Seeing Mom asleep beside her makes the voice feel wrong within the opening few seconds. The repeated cough is the strongest escalation because it suggests the thing is listening to the room right now, rather than merely using a familiar voice.

## 5. Captions OFF: which parts of the threat would still be clear from images and sound alone?

The moving handle, closed door, sleeping mother, frightened daughter, and intercom voice would still establish an unwanted presence trying to get in. The two matching coughs would make the imitation especially clear. Nia covering her mouth and then exposing it would suggest that making a sound is dangerous.

With the spoken narration still audible, the midnight rule, final-two-word recording, invitation unlock, and dawn reset would also remain understandable without captions or reading the screen. From the visuals and nonverbal sounds alone, those precise rules would be hard to infer; I would understand the intrusion and mimicry, but not exactly how speech opens the door.

## 6. Would I keep watching to the end? (1–5) and why

**5/5.** The pause lands just before a potentially disastrous first reply, and I want to hear what she says and see whether the lock reacts. I suspect a wording trap, but I’m still uncertain whether she will recognize it in time.
