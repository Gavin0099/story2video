## 1. What is the main conflict or threat?
Something outside Nia’s locked door is imitating her mother and may get inside if Nia’s first reply leaves an invitation in the intercom’s recording.

## 2. The rules of this world as I understand them, and anything that seems inconsistent
- After midnight, the intercom saves only the final two words of your first reply.
- If those saved words form an invitation, the door unlocks.
- You cannot replace that recording until dawn, so a frightened response could have lasting consequences.
- The thing can imitate Mom’s voice and even repeat a cough she makes inside the apartment. The locked door is still holding against its attempts to turn the handle.
- Silence seems to protect Nia, which explains why she covers her mouth.

I am unsure what counts as a “reply”: deliberate speech into the intercom, anything Nia says nearby, or speech from anyone in the room. Mom’s cough does not visibly use up the recording, so apparently not every sound counts. I also do not know whether the thing hears through the intercom or has some other access to the room. Its ability to copy an indoor cough makes the closed door feel like an incomplete barrier. Nothing is clearly contradictory yet, but those boundaries matter.

## 3. What I expect or fear will happen next, with my reasoning
I fear Nia will blurt out a warning whose final two words accidentally invite the thing inside. “Don’t come in” is the sort of natural frightened response that would leave “come in” after the beginning is discarded. The unusually specific two-word rule makes me expect a trap involving the difference between what she means and what the machine keeps.

Her hand slipping down suggests she is about to speak, although she has not actually said anything yet. I would also worry that Mom might wake up and answer the familiar voice without understanding the danger. Alternatively, Nia could realize the trap and stay silent or choose words that leave no invitation. I cannot yet tell whether the copied cough is just intimidation or preparation for a more convincing attempt to make her respond.

## 4. When did unease start, and what caused it?
Immediately, when the handle moves without Nia touching it. The voice calling from the intercom while Mom sleeps beside her turns that unease into a specific fear: something is pretending to be someone safe. The repeated cough is the strongest escalation because it suggests the thing can hear what is happening inside right now.

## 5. Captions OFF: which parts of the threat would still be clear from images and sound alone?
The moving handle, the sleeping mother beside the talking intercom, Nia covering her mouth, and the precisely repeated cough would still communicate an intruder, imitation, and fear of making a sound. The harder handle movement would show increasing pressure, and her uncovered mouth would feel dangerous.

With the spoken narration still audible, the two-word recording rule, invitation-triggered unlocking, and dawn reset would also remain understandable without captions. Without that narration or reading the physical screen, the images and sound effects alone would not explain why particular words can unlock the door. Her hand dropping would then suggest panic more clearly than a specific verbal trap.

## 6. Would I keep watching to the end? (1–5) and why.
5/5. I want to hear whether she speaks and exactly which two words survive. The pause lands at a clear point of danger, and the cough has made the imitation feel close and invasive. My main uncertainty is how broadly the intercom defines a reply; I would watch for whether the outcome follows the rules already given.
