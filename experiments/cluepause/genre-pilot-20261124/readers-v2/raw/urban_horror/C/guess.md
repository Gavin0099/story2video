# 1. What is the main conflict or threat?

Something outside Nia’s locked door is copying her mother’s voice and sounds, and Nia must avoid giving a reply whose final two words invite it inside.

# 2. The rules of this world as I understand them, and anything that seems inconsistent

- After midnight, the intercom records the first reply and saves only its final two words.
- If those saved words form an invitation, the door unlocks. The recording cannot be replaced until dawn.
- The intercom appears to be listening without anyone pressing a button. Staying silent seems to keep the door locked for now.
- The thing can copy sounds made inside the apartment, including Mom’s cough, almost immediately. A locked door does not prevent it from hearing them.
- I’m unsure what counts as a reply: only Nia answering deliberately, anyone speaking nearby, or even an involuntary sound? Mom’s cough is copied, but the display never clearly shows whether that uses the first reply. That is an ambiguity rather than a definite contradiction.
- The interface treats this frightening behavior like an ordinary device feature. I don’t know why these rules exist or how Nia knows them. I also don’t know whether the thing can eventually force the latch, although it has failed so far.

# 3. What I expect or fear will happen next, with my reasoning

I fear Nia will blurt out a refusal that ends with inviting words—for example, “Don’t come in.” Saving only “come in” would turn her attempt to protect herself into permission. The emphasis on the final two words makes that verbal trap feel especially likely.

Her hand slipping makes an accidental answer feel imminent, but she has not actually spoken yet. She could still catch herself and stay silent, or deliberately choose a reply with a safe ending. I would also worry that Mom might wake up and answer the familiar voice before Nia can stop her, if the intercom accepts someone else’s reply.

# 4. When did unease start, and what caused it?

At the opening: the handle turns without Nia touching it, and Mom’s voice comes through the intercom while Mom is visibly asleep nearby. The repeated cough makes it worse because the imitation is happening live; the thing seems able to listen inside their home.

# 5. Captions OFF: which parts of the threat would still be clear from images and sound alone?

The moving handle, engaged latch, Nia covering her mouth, sleeping Mom, and repeated cough would communicate an intruder, imitation, and fear of making a sound. With the spoken narration still audible, the midnight, two-word, invitation, and dawn rules would also remain understandable without captions.

Without relying on either written text or the narration’s explanation, I would understand that something is trying to get in and copying Mom, but not the precise rule about saving two words or unlocking through an invitation. Her lowered hand would suggest danger, though the image alone would not explain exactly why speaking is dangerous.

# 6. Would I keep watching to the end? (1–5) and why

**5/5.** The remaining question is immediate and specific: can she control what she says, and will the intercom twist it into permission? I want to hear her first words and see whether the latch moves.
