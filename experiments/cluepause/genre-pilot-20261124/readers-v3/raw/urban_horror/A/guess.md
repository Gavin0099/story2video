# First-time viewer impressions

## 1. What is the main conflict or threat?
Something outside Nia’s locked door is copying her sleeping mother’s voice and sounds, apparently trying to make Nia say an invitation that the intercom will save and use to unlock the door.

## 2. The rules of this world as I understand them, and anything that seems inconsistent
- After midnight, the intercom records your first reply but retains only its final two English words.
- If those saved words constitute an invitation, the door unlocks. You cannot replace the recording until dawn.
- Staying silent appears to keep the door locked, even while something turns the handle from outside.
- The thing can copy sounds made inside the apartment: it repeats Mom’s cough almost immediately. That makes the intercom feel like it is listening into the room.
- I am unsure what counts as a reply, who must speak, or how the system decides that words are an invitation. Could Mom talking in her sleep count? Would an accidental gasp use up the recording?
- “Nobody had pressed the intercom” makes its activation suspicious, although I cannot tell whether that means nobody inside answered or nobody outside called.
- The door is currently locked, yet its screen says “LATCH: SAVED CONSENT” while the first reply is still waiting. I take that as a label explaining how the latch works, but it could also look like consent has already been saved.
- The English-word restriction makes the exact wording feel crucial. I do not yet know whether refusing in another language would work.

## 3. What I expect or fear will happen next, with reasoning
My strongest expectation is that Nia will blurt out a refusal whose last two words sound like permission on their own. Something like “Don’t come in” would leave “come in,” which seems like the trap the two-word rule is setting up. Her hand slipping below her mouth makes an involuntary response feel imminent.

I also fear that the copied voice will provoke her into answering instinctively, or that Mom will wake and answer before Nia can stop her. The repeated cough suggests the threat can hear them, so even speaking quietly may be dangerous.

A possible escape is deliberately making the first reply end in an unmistakable refusal, using up the recording safely until dawn. I would be wondering why she cannot do that, unless the system interprets words unpredictably or she is too frightened to choose them carefully.

## 4. When did unease start, and what caused it?
At the first handle movement, before Nia touches it: someone is already testing the door. The voice from the intercom paired with Mom visibly asleep makes that unease supernatural almost immediately. The copied cough is the biggest escalation because it shows the thing responding to something happening inside their home, rather than merely using a familiar voice.

## 5. Captions OFF: which parts of the threat would still be clear from images and sound alone?
With the spoken audio still on, almost all the essential threat remains clear: the narrator explains the first-reply rule, the final two words, invitation unlocking, and the dawn limit. The rattling handle, sleeping mother, repeated cough, and Nia covering her mouth reinforce those explanations.

From the visuals and nonverbal sounds alone, I would understand that something is trying to enter, that the intercom echoes Mom, and that Nia thinks making a sound is dangerous. I would not reliably infer the exact two-word rule or the recording limit without narration or readable screen text. The restriction to English words is also clearer in the Chinese subtitle than in the spoken explanation.

## 6. Would I keep watching to the end? (1–5) and why.
**4/5.** I want to hear her first words and find out whether my “Don’t come in” prediction is right. The silence at the pause makes her next sound feel consequential. The detailed device rules require some concentration, and the final question repeats a danger I already understand, but the word trap gives me a concrete reason to stay.
