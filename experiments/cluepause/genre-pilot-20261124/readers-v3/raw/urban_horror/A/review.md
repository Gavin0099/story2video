# Review

## 1. Were the world rules consistent from start to end?
Yes, in the actual sequence of events. The setup says, “After midnight, it saved your first reply, but only the final two words” (7–12), followed by “An invitation unlocked the door. You couldn’t record again until dawn” (12–16). At 28–31, “Mom, don’t come in” becomes “Come in,” and the latch retracts. That follows the established mechanism exactly. The later playback uses the same recording, so it does not contradict the recording limit.

One interface ambiguity remains: “FIRST REPLY: WAITING” (7–12) appears before “LATCH: SAVED CONSENT” (12–16), while the door is still locked. “SAVED CONSENT” could look like a current status rather than a description of the latch’s operating rule. This is confusing wording, not a demonstrated contradiction in the action.

## 2. Did the ending introduce any NEW rule or setup that was necessary but not shown before?
No necessary new rule. The setup already supplies both the truncation and its consequence.

The ending adds playback: “The intercom then plays her same recorded voice: ‘Come in.’ The latch clicks only after ‘in’” (28–31). The setup did not explicitly establish playback or its timing, but this makes the saved invitation audible; the outcome does not require a new supernatural permission. The final repeated “Come in” (35–39) is likewise an added behavior, not a new condition needed to explain the breach.

## 3. Critical defects
**No confirmed rule break or unearned ending. The main defect is an over-signaled reveal.**

- **The setup exposes almost the entire twist.** “RETENTION: LAST 2 WORDS” (7–12) plus “An invitation unlocked the door” (12–16) strongly points toward a negated invitation losing its negation. My locked prediction in guess.md specifically anticipated “Don’t come in” becoming “come in.” The reveal at 28–31—“INPUT: Mom, don't come in” followed by “SAVED: Come in”—therefore confirms the obvious trap without adding another discovery. That is evidence of predictability for this viewer, not proof every viewer would guess it.
- **The fatal response has a weak immediate trigger.** At 24–28 she is already aware of the danger, and the pause contains “Low room tone; no speech.” The next beat is simply “Nia, reflexively: ‘Mom, don’t come in’” (28–31). Panic makes this possible, but nothing newly prompts her to address the voice as Mom when she has seen her mother asleep inside. The required mistake feels selected to demonstrate the word trick more than compelled by this moment.

The door staging itself is coherent: the latch retracts, the door opens inward, and Nia braces it. There is no need to invent a physical continuity error.

## 4. Tension: how did it build and where did it drop?
The opening builds tension immediately through the moving handle and the mother’s impossible voice. The 7–16 rule explanation slows the physical action and shifts attention toward solving a language puzzle. The copied cough at 16–20 restores unease by showing that the threat can respond to sounds inside the home. The harder handle movement and exposed mouth then give her next words clear stakes.

“Could she still keep it out?” (24–26) adds little because the danger is already clear. The silent hold at 26–28 does create anticipation, but for me it is anticipation of the expected phrase. The latch release at 28–31 is the strongest payoff beat. Physical struggle sustains urgency afterward, although 35–39 adds no new development beyond the door pushing back. The final replay is chilling reinforcement with limited surprise.

## 5. Did the ending pay off the suspense built earlier? (1–5)
**3/5 — ordinary, with a solid audiovisual payoff.** The saved words, playback, and latch movement clearly deliver the promised danger. Ending on Nia physically resisting the invitation’s consequence is a fair horror endpoint. However, the exact verbal trap was predictable, and the final struggle does not deepen what the viewer has learned. It pays off the mechanism more strongly than the mystery surrounding the copied mother.
