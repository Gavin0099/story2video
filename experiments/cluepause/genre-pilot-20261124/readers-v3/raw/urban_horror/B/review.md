# 1. Were the world rules consistent from start to end?
Yes. No explicit contradiction.

The setup says, “After midnight, it saved your first reply, but only the final two words,” followed by “An invitation unlocked the door. You couldn’t record again until dawn.” At 28–31, “Mom, don’t come in” becomes “Come in,” and the latch retracts. That follows the established rule precisely. The earlier cough is copied rather than saved as a verbal reply; nothing in the ending contradicts that distinction.

Unlocking does not guarantee immediate entry, so Nia physically holding the door is also consistent. The video ends before the dawn restriction can be tested.

# 2. Did the ending introduce a necessary new rule or setup?
None.

The reveal newly makes playback explicit: “The intercom then plays her same recorded voice: ‘Come in.’” At 35–39 it repeats that recording again. Automatic playback and repetition were not explicitly established, but neither is a necessary new escape clause or condition for the outcome. Saving an invitation and unlocking the door were already established.

# 3. Critical defects
- **The central reveal is heavily telegraphed.** At 7–12, “only the final two words,” combined with 12–16, “An invitation unlocked the door,” makes a negated invitation an obvious trap. The reveal at 28–31—“Mom, don’t come in” becoming “Come in”—is exactly the example I independently predicted before reading the ending. That is direct evidence that this viewer solved the mechanism during the setup. It weakens the surprise, although it can still support dread.

No definite rule break, confusing door movement, or unearned supernatural mechanism. There is a smaller motivation weakness: at 28–31, “Nia, reflexively: ‘Mom, don’t come in.’” She knows Mom is beside her and has already covered her mouth to avoid answering. Panic makes this possible, but the final delivery needs to sell the lapse; otherwise her wording feels selected to demonstrate the trap.

# 4. Tension: build and drops
The opening combines an immediate physical threat with a voice that cannot be coming from the sleeping mother. Nia covering her mouth adds anticipation: speech itself seems dangerous.

At 7–16, the recording explanation gives the threat understandable stakes, but it also slows the action and exposes the likely solution to the puzzle. The copied cough at 16–20 is the strongest escalation because it shows the thing responding to events inside the room. The harder handle movement, slipping hand, and silence at 20–28 concentrate tension on her next words.

At 28–31, the recording and latch click deliver a clear consequence. The physical struggle at 31–35 briefly raises the stakes again. At 35–39, tension levels off: she continues bracing the door and the intercom repeats the already understood invitation. The final image sustains danger but adds little new uncertainty beyond whether her strength will hold.

# 5. Did the ending pay off the suspense?
**3/5 — ordinary, but earned.**

The feared speech mistake causes a visible, audible failure of the lock. That pays off the setup clearly. However, it is the precise mistake I expected, and the remaining struggle does not deepen or complicate it. Ending on her bracing the door leaves the survival question open; it resolves how the threat gets access, rather than whether she can keep it out.

**Overall: 78/100.** A clear, coherent threat and effective mimicry create dread, but the obvious verbal trap and static final beat limit the payoff.
