# 1. What is the main conflict or threat?

Something outside Nia’s locked door is copying her mother’s voice and trying to make Nia say an invitation that the intercom will use to unlock the door.

# 2. The rules of this world as I understand them, and anything inconsistent

- After midnight, the intercom records your first reply, retaining only its final two words.
- If those saved words form an invitation, the door unlocks. The recording cannot be replaced until dawn, so a verbal mistake could be irreversible for the night.
- The thing can imitate Mom even while she is visibly asleep, and can copy a cough she has just made inside the apartment. The locked door still physically holds against its attempts to turn the handle.
- Nia seems to know that speaking is dangerous: she covers her mouth and avoids the buttons. I assume it can hear a reply without her pressing anything.

The biggest uncertainty is what counts as a “reply” or an “invitation.” Would a gasp, cough, or speech directed at Mom count? Mom’s cough is copied but apparently does not consume the first reply; perhaps only spoken words count, or only Nia’s response to the caller. I also cannot tell whether the saved two words are interpreted without the rest of the sentence, though the emphasis on retention strongly suggests that trap. Nothing has plainly contradicted the rules yet, but those boundaries are unclear.

# 3. What I expect or fear will happen next, with reasoning

My strongest expectation is that Nia will blurt out something like “Don’t come in.” The device would save “come in,” turning a refusal into an invitation. The unusually specific two-word rule and the shot of her hand slipping away from her mouth make me expect a language trap rather than the door simply breaking.

I also fear that the imitation will use Mom’s voice to provoke an automatic answer, especially now that it has demonstrated it can hear sounds inside. Alternatively, Nia might deliberately give a first reply ending in something safely uninviting, using the one-recording limit to protect herself until dawn. I do not yet know whether the device would accept that or whether someone else speaking could undermine it.

# 4. When did unease start, and what caused it?

At the opening handle movement: someone is testing the door while Nia’s hand is visibly clear of it. The cut to Mom asleep while her voice comes through the intercom makes that unease specifically supernatural. The copied cough is the strongest escalation because it suggests the thing is listening to the room right now, rather than merely playing an old recording.
