# 1. Were the world rules consistent from start to end?

Yes. The setup says, “After midnight, it saved your first reply, but only the final two words” (7–12 s) and “An invitation unlocked the door. You couldn’t record again until dawn” (12–16 s). Nia’s first spoken reply is “Mom, don’t come in,” whose final two words are “come in.” The device saves and plays those words, and the latch retracts (28–31 s). That follows the stated mechanism.

Unlocking does not guarantee entry: Nia can still physically brace the door. Her struggle at 31–39 s therefore does not contradict the invitation rule. The dawn restriction is not tested within this ending.

Inconsistency: none demonstrated. The setup’s uncertainty about what qualifies as a reply remains, but the ending uses an ordinary spoken response and does not require resolving that uncertainty.

# 2. Did the ending introduce any necessary new rule or setup?

No necessary new rule. Treating the retained words as an invitation independently of the full sentence is the reveal, but the combination of “only the final two words” and “An invitation unlocked the door” already supports it.

The ending adds audible playback and later repetition: “One low intercom replay in Nia’s recorded voice: ‘Come in’” (35–39 s). Neither behavior was explicitly established earlier, but neither is necessary to make the unlocking work. The replay mainly makes the result legible and supplies a final sound motif.

# 4. Tension: how did it build and where did it drop?

The moving handle and Mom’s voice while she is visibly asleep establish danger immediately (0–3.5 s). Nia covering her mouth adds a behavioral mystery (3.5–7 s). The recording rules explain why one reflexive sentence could be dangerous (7–16 s), although this extended explanation briefly slows the immediate threat.

The copied cough is the strongest escalation (16–20 s): the mimic appears able to hear the room and respond in real time. The harder handle jerk and Nia exposing her mouth bring the danger back to her next action (20–24 s).

“Could she still keep it out?” (24–26 s) repeats the existing question rather than adding pressure. The subsequent silence can create anticipation, but the language trap was already apparent to me: my locked prediction named the exact phrase “Don’t come in.”

At 28–31 s, the retained words and retracting latch deliver a clear, immediate consequence. Tension then changes from avoiding a verbal mistake to resisting physical entry. The opening door and shoulder impact give that change weight (31–35 s). It levels off at 35–39 s because the final beat continues the same bracing action and repeats “Come in” without a further discovery or decisive change.

# 5. Did the ending pay off the earlier suspense? (1–5)

**3/5 — ordinary, with effective execution.** The central mechanism pays off cleanly: a refusal becomes an invitation, and the lock visibly responds. Hearing Nia’s own voice admit the threat is a good reversal of the earlier imitation of Mom.

However, this is almost exactly the outcome I predicted before seeing the reveal. The ending confirms the trap rather than adding a surprising implication. The final struggle preserves danger but leaves the initial question—whether she can keep it out—unanswered. That is a workable horror cutoff, though less satisfying than the strong setup could support.
