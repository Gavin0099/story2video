**Premise retained:** Shoes put away dry become wet each morning inside a locked cabinet. A fault in the plant's timed watering line wets their soles from inside; nobody wore the shoes outside.

**Author-only causal summary:** The watering hose loops through a rear vent; a connector inside that loop splits under pump pressure. Water runs down the inner rear wall into the shallow shoe tray. When the tray reaches its low front lip, water spills onto the cabinet floor and out through the bottom seam. The plant saucer's mineral ring and the old exterior streak suggest overflow from above, but this cycle leaves paper under the saucer dry. Water escapes inside the hose circuit, below the cabinet top.

## Revision rationale — author / host only

All three v2 readers inferred a morning hose leak (85–90%) after the timer click and the narrated hose-through-vent sequence. v3 stops naming that route and delays the six-o'clock click until the reveal. The watering equipment remains visible in the setup. Its pale connector is already visible at 3.5–7 s and again during the evening reset; the reveal reveals its pressure-dependent fault, not a new component.

The alternative is specific: the plant saucer overflows during watering, and water runs down from above through the rear vent. A real tide mark and dried exterior streak support it, while pooled water and the dry hallway fit either that route or an internal leak. Neither observation proves the current source. Her white sheet tests the overflow hypothesis without resolving it before the pause. At the end, its dry surface rules out that route for this cycle while the connector visibly releases water below it. The old streak becomes misleading history rather than a current water trail; the lock was guarding an irrelevant entrance.

Keep all events chronological: morning discovery, evening reset, next morning observation. The timer's six-o'clock schedule is confirmed at the reveal rather than announced as a pre-pause answer cue. A real slack hose passes through the vent and back up to the plant, with the connector inside, below the top paper and above the tray. The connector must be the same shape and position in all shots; a hairline split may be invisible while unpressurized. Show no bead or active water path before 27 s. Do not introduce a new hose, hidden person or supernatural tail.

The shallow tray and its low lip appear before the reveal. The final insert is after the pump cycle, not a claim that one drop instantly fills a tray. Show water crossing its lip and the cabinet floor to the opening seam so the first drip receives a visible explanation. The sheet covers the saucer footprint and the top-to-rear route; do not use an uncovered gap to let the rejected overflow explanation survive. These staging choices need future visual verification.
