**Premise:** Retained: a fictional night intercom stores only the last two words of the first reply. An imitation of her mother provokes “Mom, don’t come in”; the stored invitation opens the lock. Mother is now asleep on the nearby sofa rather than upstairs, making the voice contradiction visible immediately.

**Reveal / author-only causal summary:** The first reply is “Mom, don’t come in.” The device discards the prefix and loops “come in,” releasing the latch. The thing outside gains an invitation, but the door remains a physical barrier that Nia can brace.

## Author / host notes — never distribute to blind readers

This is invented supernatural fiction, not a claim about real intercom security. Rules remain: after midnight, only the first reply is stored and only its final two English words survive; an invitation releases the latch and loops in the resident’s voice; it cannot be overwritten before dawn. The outside presence can imitate a voice it hears, cannot cross the locked threshold without invitation, and has no teleportation or possession ability. A released latch does not remove the door. Mother is inside throughout. The immediate copying of her cough gives the voice a concrete disturbing capability, without explaining the entity’s identity.

The rules at 7–16 s are necessary fair constraints, not the solution event. Do not name, quote, demonstrate or subtitle “come in” or Nia’s eventual refusal in any pre-reveal material, including the device screen. The status states retention, saved consent and dawn replacement without an example invitation, highlighted phrase or suggested refusal. The physical status and sound readout are in-world evidence; no detached rule cards, word-removal animation or predictive latch sound. The input and saved-word readout appear only at the reveal, in sync with the actual utterance/playback; never prepopulate them. Her first actual reply begins at the current 28 s design point, after a suspense hold whose length may change. Capture that complete utterance, then reuse exactly its last two spoken words in playback; do not swap to another voice. English and Traditional Chinese sidecars at the reveal retain the actual English phrases so the two-English-word mechanism survives translation.

Fair evidence before 26 s: the voice calls at 0.2 s, the handle turns without anyone inside touching it at 0.35 s, and Mom is visibly asleep in the opening shot; Nia tries to remain silent at 3.5 s; the truncation, invitation and one-reply rules are spoken and visible as device status at 7–16 s; a matched cough at 17.2/18.0 s and its device readout establish imitation; the handle jerks harder but the latch holds at 20 s. The reveal connects her reflexive familiar-address warning to those rules, and her own recorded voice becomes the threat. Readers may predict a verbal trap; do not punish prediction itself.

The chief unresolved editorial risk is whether Nia’s startle-induced warning feels plausible after she has tried to stay silent. It is an involuntary response, not a deliberate plan to beat the system; stage the outside handle movement as its trigger. Do not claim that resolves reader objections. Host checks: can readers restate the first-reply/last-two-words consequence, hear the refusal become an invitation, and connect the braced door to the lost lock? No unique monster identity or escape solution is required. Keep genre evaluation pending, including character credibility; no gore, injury or jump-scare-only payoff.
