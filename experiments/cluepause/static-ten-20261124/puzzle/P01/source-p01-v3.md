**Premise retained:** A buyer cuts a thread beneath a jeweler's mechanical spring scale. The reading drops although the same three gold pieces remain. The seller is deliberately cheating; this is not `benevolent_misunderstanding`.

**Author-only causal summary:** The thread is knotted to an eyelet beneath the moving pan, passes through the counter and behind a brass calibration weight, then loops around the seller's toe. Pressing his toe down supplies extra downward force while both hands remain above the counter. The brass weight stands independently on a shelf; it hides the lower thread rather than hanging from it.

## Revision rationale — author / host only

The v2 readers' leading guesses were the seller's hand pulling a thread (88–92%). v3 removes the consecutive hidden-fist and stopped-counting clues. Both hands are now visibly on the counter during the higher reading. A weight-shaped object aligned below the thread and a stable reading supply positive evidence for an alternative: a fixed hanging ballast adds the extra load, without ongoing manipulation. That is a possible inference, never a narrator's assertion that the weight is attached.

The reveal overturns the ballast explanation by showing the cylinder supported on a shelf and its ring empty. It reinterprets the cylinder as concealment, the apparently reassuring hands as a distraction, and the lifted heel as the source of force. The attachment to the moving pan is already visible at 7–11 s, without a narrated explanation or a force demonstration. A viewer may still infer a foot pull; this design is not a measured improvement until new readers test it.

The opening and 23–27 s are after the cut; 3.5–23 s and 27–32 s revisit the intact arrangement. “Earlier” marks the first return in speech. Preserve the same pan dent, gold, cylinder, shelf, thread route and shoe. The spring pan has clearance below it and moves independently of the counter; the thread acts on the pan, never merely on the scale housing. The shelf is already present in the low views, while the cylinder's contact with it is outside the 11–15 s crop. Do not float the cylinder, show a false attachment, or introduce a second thread. Both hypotheses must fit the actual pre-pause images. The toe loop and its tension are revealed at 27 s; no new device is introduced.
