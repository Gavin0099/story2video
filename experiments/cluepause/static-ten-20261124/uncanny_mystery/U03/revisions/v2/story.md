## Story

A head-and-shoulders silhouette stands in a closed frosted pantry door each evening. An empty coat hanger inside suggests a hanging coat. But the pantry is empty when opened. A small counter lamp projects the combined shadow of a round perforated colander and a navy tea towel on the dish rack onto the outside of the glass. Moving the colander breaks the head away from the shoulders.

Plausible alternative before the reveal: A coat on the pantry’s wall hook makes a person-shaped silhouette. The visible hanger gives this a physical basis; opening the door reveals an empty hanger, and the shadow can be separated by moving objects outside the pantry.

Original author draft. Optical projection differs from water and mechanical towing. The entire shadow source stays OUTSIDE the pantry throughout: lamp, rack/towel, colander, then frosted pane in that order. No impossible person or actual translucent ghost. The setup allows the coat/hanger hypothesis without inventing an intruder. Tiny shadow perforations are the fair clue. The reveal must visibly break one apparent body into two independently moved ordinary objects. No text evidence is used.
