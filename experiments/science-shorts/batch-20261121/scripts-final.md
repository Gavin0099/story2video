## 冰為什麼浮在水上？

英文標題：Why Does Ice Float?。成片 48.10 秒；文字/分鏡評分 88／89。

[繁中字幕試看片](../../../artifacts/science-shorts/batch-20261121/ice/ice-zh.mp4)

### 英文旁白

Drop an ice cube into water. Why does the solid stay on top? You might picture freezing as packing everything tighter. Here, the arrangement opens up. Compare the liquid and frozen forms, using the same amount of water. As ordinary ice forms, the molecules settle into a more open, ordered pattern. That pattern leaves more space between molecules than in the liquid. Keep the same amount of water, and the frozen version takes up more room. Same mass, larger volume. That means a lower density. Ordinary ice is less dense than the liquid water around it, so it floats. The trick is inside the cube: how the water molecules are arranged.

### 繁中字幕

把冰塊放進水裡，為什麼固體反而浮在上面？你可能以為結冰會讓分子排得更緊，這裡的排列卻變得更鬆。拿同樣多的水，比較液態與結冰後的樣子。普通的冰形成時，分子會排成較開放、規則的結構。這種排列讓分子之間的空間，比液態時更多。水的量相同，結成冰以後，佔的空間卻更大。同樣的質量，體積更大，密度就更低。普通冰的密度比周圍的液態水低，所以會浮起來。關鍵藏在冰塊裡，就是水分子的排列方式。

- [USGS — Water Density](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density)
- [IAPWS — FAQs About Water and Steam](https://iapws.org/faqs/faq1)

適用範圍：Ordinary water ice in liquid water at everyday pressure; compare equal masses. Excludes other ice phases, heavy water, ecological effects, and numerical floating fractions.

## 天空為什麼是藍色？

英文標題：Why Is the Sky Blue?。成片 46.10 秒；文字/分鏡評分 87／86。

[繁中字幕試看片](../../../artifacts/science-shorts/batch-20261121/sky/sky-zh.mp4)

### 英文旁白

Why is the sky blue when sunlight looks white? The light starts out carrying many colors mixed together. To see what happens next, follow it into Earth's atmosphere. Tiny air molecules scatter some of that light in different directions. They scatter blue light more strongly than red light. So blue light reaches your eyes from across the sky, away from the Sun. Now move the Sun toward the horizon in our diagram. Sunlight takes a longer path through the atmosphere to reach the observer. More blue is scattered out of that direct path, leaving the light looking redder. Blue sky and red sunset: the same scattering, with a different path through air.

### 繁中字幕

陽光看起來是白色，天空為什麼卻是藍色？原來陽光裡，混合了許多不同顏色的光。接著，跟著這束光進入地球的大氣。微小的空氣分子，會把一部分光散射到不同方向。其中，藍光比紅光更容易被散射。因此，看向太陽以外的天空，也有藍光從不同方向進入眼睛。現在，把示意圖中的太陽移到接近地平線。陽光要穿過更長一段大氣，才到得了觀察者。更多藍光在途中被散射出去，沿原路到達的光就顯得更紅。藍天與紅色夕陽，都是散射，只是光穿過大氣的路徑不同。

### 來源

- [NASA — Why Is the Sky Blue?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/)
- [NOAA/NWS — Why Is The Sky Blue?](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/SkyBlue)

適用範圍：Clear Earth sky explained through molecular scattering; compare blue with red only. A sunset diagram changes the light path to reinforce the same mechanism, without claiming a complete account of every sky color.

## 隔著真空，太空人怎麼通話？

英文標題：How Do Astronauts Talk Across Empty Space?。成片 51.80 秒；文字/分鏡評分 89／87。

[繁中字幕試看片](../../../artifacts/science-shorts/batch-20261121/space/space-zh.mp4)

### 英文旁白

Two astronauts. Empty space between them. How can either hear a word? Shouting louder seems like the obvious answer. But sound needs a carrier. A voice makes matter vibrate, passing a disturbance from particle to particle. Air can carry that disturbance. A perfect vacuum has no matter to carry it. So the voice itself cannot cross the empty gap between them. Their radios solve this by sending electromagnetic waves instead of sound waves. These waves can cross a vacuum without air or other material carrying them. The receiving radio turns the signal back into sound through a speaker. That is what reaches the astronaut's ears. Same message, different carrier: radio across the gap, sound at the receiving end.

### 繁中字幕

兩名太空人，中間隔著真空，怎麼還能聽見對方說話？直覺會想喊大聲一點，但聲音得靠物質傳遞。說話讓物質振動，把擾動從一個粒子傳到下一個。空氣能傳遞這種擾動；理想真空裡沒有物質能接力。所以，聲音本身無法穿過兩人之間的真空。無線電解決了這個問題：送出去的是電磁波。電磁波可以穿過真空，不需要空氣或其他物質承載。接收端的無線電，再透過喇叭把訊號轉回聲音。最後，這段聲音才進入太空人的耳朵。同一句話，換了傳法：跨越真空靠無線電，接收後再變回聲音。

### 來源

- [NASA — Anatomy of an Electromagnetic Wave](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/02_anatomy/)
- [ESA — LISTEN UP!](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/listen-up)
- [NASA — Radio Waves](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/05_radiowaves/)

適用範圍：Two separated astronauts communicating across an idealized vacuum gap, with no solid connection. Distinguish mechanical sound from electromagnetic radio; do not generalize silence to all locations in the universe.
