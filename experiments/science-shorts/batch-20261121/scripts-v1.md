## ice: Why Does Ice Float? / 冰為什麼浮在水上？

111 English words; 9 cues; 3.1 seconds of provisional pauses; estimated 50.7 seconds at 140 words/minute.

Question: Why does ordinary ice float on liquid water?

Scope: Ordinary water ice in liquid water at everyday pressure; compare equal masses. Excludes other ice phases, heavy water, ecological effects, and numerical floating fractions.

### English voiceover — complete

Drop an ice cube into water. Why does the solid stay on top? You might picture freezing as packing everything tighter. Here, the arrangement opens up. Compare the liquid and frozen forms, using the same amount of water. As ordinary ice forms, the molecules settle into a more open, ordered pattern. That pattern leaves more space between molecules than in the liquid. Keep the same amount of water, and the frozen version takes up more room. Same mass, larger volume. That means a lower density. Ordinary ice is less dense than the liquid water around it, so it floats. The trick is inside the cube: how the water molecules are arranged.

### Claim evidence

- c1: Ordinary ice has a more open, ordered molecular arrangement than liquid water, with more space between molecules. [Source](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density); [Source](https://iapws.org/faqs/faq1)
- c2: When the same mass of water occupies a larger volume as ordinary ice, its density is lower. [Source](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density); [Source](https://iapws.org/faqs/faq1)
- c3: Ordinary ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water. [Source](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density); [Source](https://iapws.org/faqs/faq1)

Core answer: Ordinary ice has a more open molecular structure, so the same mass takes more volume, has lower density than liquid water, and floats.

### Visual constraints

- All visuals must be original programmatic diagrams; do not download source images.
- Molecule diagrams are schematic: label them and do not imply a flat two-dimensional physical crystal.
- Use equal molecule counts and identical molecule sizes in equal-mass comparisons; expansion is spacing, not growing molecules or added air.
- Show most of the floating cube submerged, without a numerical submerged fraction.
- Keep English narration and editable Traditional Chinese captions separate from scientific diagrams.

### Misconceptions to avoid

- Do not imply that being solid alone makes a material float.
- Do not attribute ordinary ice floating to trapped air.
- Do not generalize this explanation to all ice phases or all liquids.
- The script describes the arrangement without naming hydrogen bonds, because the paired USGS page does not explicitly name that bond.

## sky: Why Is the Sky Blue? / 天空為什麼是藍色？

112 English words; 10 cues; 3.6 seconds of provisional pauses; estimated 51.6 seconds at 140 words/minute.

Question: Why does Earth's clear daytime sky look blue?

Scope: Clear Earth sky explained through molecular scattering; compare blue with red only. A sunset diagram changes the light path to reinforce the same mechanism, without claiming a complete account of every sky color.

### English voiceover — complete

Why is the sky blue when sunlight looks white? The light starts out carrying many colors mixed together. To see what happens next, follow it into Earth's atmosphere. Tiny air molecules scatter some of that light in different directions. They scatter blue light more strongly than red light. So blue light reaches your eyes from across the sky, away from the Sun. Now move the Sun toward the horizon in our diagram. Sunlight takes a longer path through the atmosphere to reach the observer. More blue is scattered out of that direct path, leaving the light looking redder. Blue sky and red sunset: the same scattering, with a different path through air.

### 繁中字幕全文

陽光看起來是白色，天空為什麼卻是藍色？原來陽光裡，混合了許多不同顏色的光。接著，跟著這束光進入地球的大氣。微小的空氣分子，會把一部分光散射到不同方向。其中，藍光比紅光更容易被散射。因此，看向太陽以外的天空，也有藍光從不同方向進入眼睛。現在，把示意圖中的太陽移到接近地平線。陽光要穿過更長一段大氣，才到得了觀察者。更多藍光在途中被散射出去，沿原路到達的光就顯得更紅。藍天與紅色夕陽，都是散射，只是光穿過大氣的路徑不同。

### Claim evidence

- c1: Sunlight that looks white contains many visible colors. [Source](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/); [Source](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/SkyBlue)

- c3: Near sunset, sunlight follows a longer atmospheric path to the observer; more blue light is scattered away from the direct path and the transmitted light appears redder. [Source](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/); [Source](https://www.weather.gov/fgz/SkyBlue)

Core answer: Air molecules scatter blue light more strongly than red light, sending blue sunlight toward your eyes from across the clear daytime sky.

### Visual constraints

- Use original vector or canvas diagrams only; no downloaded sky photos or source graphics.
- Show colors bundled in the incoming beam: air molecules do not separate sunlight as a prism would.
- Show scattering as a change of direction, not a change of blue light into red light.
- Compare blue only against red; never label blue the most scattered color.
- Draw observer and ray paths explicitly; the sunset diagram is explanatory and is not an instruction to stare at the Sun.
- Label all path and ray diagrams as schematic; do not encode an unsupported numerical scattering ratio.

### Misconceptions to avoid

- Do not claim blue scatters more than every other visible color: violet has a shorter wavelength.
- Do not imply that scattering turns blue photons into red photons.
- Do not claim that every sunset or the whole evening sky is red.
- Do not add unverified claims about the ocean causing or not causing the sky's color.

## space: How Do Astronauts Talk Across Empty Space? / 隔著真空，太空人怎麼通話？

118 English words; 10 cues; 3.5 seconds of provisional pauses; estimated 54.1 seconds at 140 words/minute.

Question: How can two astronauts hear a message across a vacuum when sound cannot cross it?

Scope: Two separated astronauts communicating across an idealized vacuum gap, with no solid connection. Distinguish mechanical sound from electromagnetic radio; do not generalize silence to all locations in the universe.

### English voiceover — complete

Two astronauts. Empty space between them. How can either hear a word? Shouting louder seems like the obvious answer. But sound needs a carrier. A voice makes matter vibrate, passing a disturbance from particle to particle. Air can carry that disturbance. A perfect vacuum has no matter to carry it. So the voice itself cannot cross the empty gap between them. Their radios solve this by sending electromagnetic waves instead of sound waves. These waves can cross a vacuum without air or other material carrying them. The receiving radio turns the signal back into sound through a speaker. That is what reaches the astronaut's ears. Same message, different carrier: radio across the gap, sound at the receiving end.

### 繁中字幕全文

兩名太空人，中間隔著真空，怎麼還能聽見對方說話？直覺會想喊大聲一點，但聲音得靠物質傳遞。說話讓物質振動，把擾動從一個粒子傳到下一個。空氣能傳遞這種擾動；理想真空裡沒有物質能接力。所以，聲音本身無法穿過兩人之間的真空。無線電解決了這個問題：送出去的是電磁波。電磁波可以穿過真空，不需要空氣或其他物質承載。接收端的無線電，再透過喇叭把訊號轉回聲音。最後，這段聲音才進入太空人的耳朵。同一句話，換了傳法：跨越真空靠無線電，接收後再變回聲音。

### Claim evidence

- c1: Sound is a mechanical disturbance carried through matter, including air; a perfect vacuum provides no material medium for that disturbance to propagate. [Source](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/02_anatomy/); [Source](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/listen-up)
- c2: Radio waves are electromagnetic waves and can propagate across a vacuum without a material medium. [Source](https://science.gsfc.nasa.gov/attic/cosmicopia.gsfc.nasa.gov/qa_sp_en.html); [Source](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/listen-up)
- c3: Receiving equipment translates a radio signal into sound through a speaker, so the message can cross the vacuum as radio and be heard locally as sound. [Source](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/05_radiowaves/); [Source](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/listen-up)

Core answer: The voice cannot cross a vacuum as sound. Radios carry the message as electromagnetic waves, then receiving equipment converts it back into audible sound.

### Visual constraints

- Use original programmatic astronaut icons and diagrams only; no NASA or ESA images or audio.
- The external gap must have no air dots, tether, shared solid surface, or traveling sound-wave animation.
- Distinguish sound and electromagnetic radio through both labels and shape, not color alone.
- Show sound particles oscillating locally rather than flying along with the wave.
- Use PERFECT VACUUM for the idealized empty-medium diagram; do not label the entire universe silent.
- Receiving equipment and local sound must be visible; radio waves do not enter the ear as audible sound.

### Misconceptions to avoid

- Louder sound cannot cross a perfect vacuum.
- Radio waves are not mechanical sound waves.
- This does not imply that sound is absent inside air-filled spacecraft or throughout the universe.
- A transmission diagram does not imply instantaneous communication.
